The five samples are SUN (February 2025 consolidation), MFG (2 March 2026 trading halt), BHP, CBA and WGX. Index membership for every historical date has not been certified; that requires the operator's dated membership authority.

The SUN result depends on two distinct code paths in `src/qat/operational/history.py`: `_derived_raw` multiplies the analytical share volume by `SplitFactor(10000, 8511)` and rejects a non-integer result; `derive_pre_split_bar` labels accepted reconstructed raw values `DERIVED`. `_bar_reasons` then checks the exact inverse but skips tick validation for `DERIVED` raw OHLC. If an adjusted SUN volume is passed through unchanged, `parse_bar` calls `_integer` and rejects it; constructing `OperationalBar` directly also calls `_integer` on both `Ohlcv` volumes in `OperationalBar.__post_init__`. `Ohlcv.volume` in `src/qat/domain/strategies/authoritative_swing/model.py` is typed `int`; its dataclass constructor alone does not enforce that type, but the operational record does. Passing a fractional `Decimal` through a directly constructed `Ohlcv` could let `_derived_raw` calculate the whole raw volume, yet `derive_pre_split_bar` reaches the `OperationalBar` integer check and fails. Passing the binary float to `Fraction` instead would violate the specified exact conversion. Thus the current operational record cannot carry these fractional **analytical** volumes. A conversion that discards the fraction would not preserve the recorded bar and was not attempted. The full 341-row derived OHLC/volume audit is in the hashed file above, outside Git.

## Paper broker capability

[IBKR describes a limit-on-open buy](https://www.interactivebrokers.com.au/en/trading/ordertypes.php?m=bracketModal) as `orderType=LMT`, `tif=OPG`, executing at the open at or below the limit and cancelling if unfilled. No other IBKR form found in the reviewed ASX documentation both has an explicit buy limit **and** expires after that opening auction: [IBKR's AUC auction order](https://ibkrcampus.com/campus/glossary-terms/at-auction-order/) may be resubmitted as an ordinary limit after the open. The sample was 9 BHP shares at AUD 61.06 (approximately AUD 550). All attempts below used the standalone script, `whatIf=True`; **none was an actual transmitted order**. The exact normal order sequence, using QAT's existing broker adapter, is: verify one DU account and no BHP position or working order; obtain the BHP quote; submit one paper BUY **MKT** for that parcel after 10:05 Sydney; verify the holding; submit a paper SELL **STP GTC** for the actual whole-share holding at the tick-rounded 90%-of-reference stop; verify it is working; modify that same stop **up one ASX tick** and verify; cancel it and verify it is absent; submit one paper SELL **MKT** for the actual whole-share holding; verify flat and no working BHP order. The DU account is rechecked before every Gateway request, including adapter calls. If an intermediate step fails, cleanup cancels pending probe orders. It never sells while a protective stop may still rest. If a holding remains **without** a working stop after cleanup, it sends one rescue paper SELL **MKT** for that exact whole-share holding; a protected holding or any unresolved state is reported for operator action. No opening-auction order is part of this sequence.

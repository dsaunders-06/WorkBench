# Moving to IBKR and the ASX — Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax.

**Goal:** Reach a state where this application can trade the ASX through an IBKR paper account with the same protection guarantees it had on Alpaca — nothing silently degraded.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-12-asx-transferable-validation-design.md`. **Read it first.** This plan implements it and corrects it where 19 August measurement disagrees.

**Trial close:** `docs/2026-08-19-us-trial-close.md` — what the US phase did and did not establish.

## THIS IS FOUR SUB-PROJECTS, NOT ONE

The spec's W1 is a feasibility spike, not an implementation. Implementing it spans four subsystems with different blockers, and forcing them into one plan would produce a plan nobody can execute.

| Stage | What | Blocked on |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The adapter can be trusted** | W1.1 measurement, the three implementable methods, the order-identity bridge | **A LIVE IBKR account with ASX permissions granted** — paper mirrors live, so there is no ASX in paper until there is ASX in live. Lead time, not a formality. |
| **2. Market data** | Which vendor supplies ASX daily bars, corporate-action adjusted, point-in-time | A commercial decision |

**Only Stage 1 is planned in detail below.** The others get their own spec then plan cycle once Stage 1's measurement says what they actually need. Planning them now would be guessing.

- **PAPER ONLY.** `LivePortInPaperModeError` already refuses a live port (4001/7496) in paper mode — W1.4, landed 12 August. Do not weaken it. The guard is deliberately one-directional: live mode against a paper port is the safe mismatch and is allowed.
- **Read-only first.** `IBAdapter` has a `read_only` mode and `_check_not_read_only`. Every W1.1 measurement runs read-only. **No order is placed on IBKR until the measurement is complete and reported.**
- Any script run outside pytest passes its own `data_dir`; `Settings(_env_file=None).data_dir` is the LIVE directory.
- Formats with `black`, never `ruff format`. Verify with `ruff check .`, `black --check .`, `mypy src`, `bandit -r src -q`, `pytest tests -q`, all via `./.venv/Scripts/python.exe`.

### ⚠️ TWO THINGS RESEARCHED 19 AUGUST THAT CHANGE THIS PLAN

**1. ASX paper trading exists, but only through the live account's permissions.**
IBKR paper accounts are mirror images of the linked live account — *"Trading
permissions, market data subscriptions, base currency, and other account
configurations are the same as specified for your regular account."* So there is
no ASX in paper until ASX is permissioned on the LIVE account, and permissions
are granted on financial profile, product knowledge, prior experience and
location.

**Stage 1 is therefore blocked on more than "the account existing".** It is
blocked on a live IBKR account with ASX trading permissions granted, and that
approval is not instantaneous. Treat it as lead time, not a formality.

**2. STOPS ARE ALWAYS SIMULATED IN PAPER TRADING, and this is the one that
threatens the design.** IBKR's own words: *"Stops and other complex order types
are always simulated in paper trading, which may result in slightly different
behavior from a production account."* Separately, in LIVE trading IBKR simulates
stops on any exchange that does not natively support them, and publishes per
exchange which it does.

A simulated stop is **held on IBKR's servers, not resting at the exchange**. It
triggers only while the primary exchange is open in regular hours with a valid
bid/ask, it is slower, and price can trade through the level before the order
arrives.

**Why that matters more here than it would elsewhere.** The US trial's single
strongest safety claim was *10 of 10 carry a stop resting at the broker*,
verified at every check across nineteen days, and `PortfolioGovernor` measures
every position's risk TO THAT STOP. If the stop is simulated:

* there is **no protection outside regular trading hours** — which is exactly
  the case the gap-risk rail exists for, and exactly how MNST lost 51.4%;
* protection depends on IBKR's market data being healthy, a dependency the
  Alpaca model did not have;
* `risk_at_stop` becomes an optimistic measure rather than a conservative one,
  and this project has already been bitten once by a risk figure that flattered
  itself (M66/M90, understating by 27%).

**NOT YET CONFIRMED, and Task 1 must settle it:** whether ASX stops are native
or IBKR-simulated in a live account. IBKR publishes this per exchange; the
order-types page returned 403 to research and the exchange listing sits behind a
search form. **Do not assume either way.**

Sources: `ibkrguides.com/clientportal/aboutpapertradingaccounts.htm`,
IBKR Campus trading lessons on paper accounts and trading permissions, and
IBKR's stock stop order disclosure (form 9130).

### The consequence that is easy to miss

`_correct_announced_price` — M70 and M71 — resolves an incoming fill through `_order_the_broker_calls(raw.order_id)`. With no identity bridge that lookup cannot match, so **both corrections go dormant on IBKR without erroring**. The app would record entry and exit prices it never paid, silently, exactly as it did before M70 was written. That is why Task 3 sits in Stage 1 rather than being deferred.

## Stages 2 to 4, outlined only

**Stage 2 — market data. RESEARCHED 19 AUGUST, and it is larger than the spec implies.**

The operator established that ASX data through IBKR is a paid subscription, about **$25/month**. The question asked was whether that subscription meets this application's needs. **It does not, as the code stands, and the reason is upstream of anything the subscription contains.**

* **There is no IBKR history path at all.** `resolve_history_source()` accepts `Literal["synthetic", "yfinance", "alpaca"]` — no `ibkr` branch, no `IBHistorySource`, and `IBAdapter.get_historical` is consumed by nothing in the strategy path (a caller search returns only docstrings saying it exists to satisfy a protocol). Every bar the system trades on arrives via `WarmStart` -> `fetch_daily_panel` -> `resolve_history_source`. **On IBKR today, bars would come from yfinance or from SYNTHETIC data.** The subscription would buy real-time quotes for `reqMktData` -> `get_market_data` (tick-driven bars, the staleness rail) and **not one bar any strategy can see.**

* **101 market data lines needed, 100 available** — confirmed below, with a one-symbol fix.

* **Historical requests are capped at 60 per 10 minutes** (also: no identical request within 15s, no six for one contract within 2s). A warm start is 101 symbols. `fetch_daily_panel` prefers a bulk call and falls back to a **sequential unpaced loop**; IBKR has no bulk historical endpoint. A naively written `IBHistorySource` trips the limit at request 61 and gets throttled or disconnected. **Design constraint, not a blocker.**

### CONFIRMED 19 AUGUST by the operator and by IBKR's own paper-trading page

**ASX data resolves.** The $25/month ASX subscription IS available to the paper
account **while the live account holds funds**, and it carries **L1 and L2**. We
need L1 only (`get_market_data` returns bid/ask/last), so depth is bundled
rather than required. **The API question is settled too**, from IBKR directly:
*"IBKR's Web API and TWS API can be used with IBKR paper accounts with minimal
differences in functionality compared to live use. Paper trades are executed by
the same simulator employed by IBKR's own platforms."*

**101 LINES NEEDED, 100 AVAILABLE, AND GOING OVER IS EXPENSIVE.** Measured:
`MARKET_WATCHLISTS["ASX"]` is exactly 100 symbols and `STW.AX` is NOT among
them, and `runtime.py:554-559` streams `(benchmark, *watchlist)` with a comment
explaining the benchmark **must** be streamed or `RegimeEngine` never receives a
`MarketDataEvent` for it and regime detection never fires.

**The fix is one symbol.** Trim the ASX watchlist to 99 so 99 + `STW.AX` = 100.
Dropping the least liquid of a hundred megacaps costs almost nothing
analytically and avoids a substantial cost step. **Do this in Stage 3, and do it
deliberately with the dropped symbol named in the commit** — a universe that
silently differs from the one the harness replayed would make the two
incomparable.

**TWO CONSEQUENCES OF THE PAPER ACCOUNT ITSELF, both from IBKR's page.**

**1. Paper accounts start with USD 1,000,000.** That is 10x the US trial's
~$100k, and it changes WHICH RAILS BIND. Percentage rails scale, but the
cost-to-risk rail does not: it refused **269 times** in the US trial, on lines
reading *"Round-trip cost $18.24 is 13.2% of the $138.38 at risk"* — a
FLOOR-DOMINATED cost (ASX floors are AUD 6.60 Fixed / 5.50 Tiered) amortised
over ten times the risk becomes roughly 1.3% against a 10% limit. **Expect
cost-to-risk to stop binding almost entirely.** An ASX paper run is therefore
NOT comparable to the US trial on rail mix, and should not be read as if it
were.

**2. Partial fills behave differently from production.** IBKR: *"The trade
simulator will reject the remainder of any exchange-directed market order that
partially executes."* M53 was a partial-fill defect that halted the first
session that ever recorded a trade, so this path is load-bearing here — and the
simulator's behaviour is explicitly not the exchange's.

### The decision Stage 2 exists to make

**Which source supplies ASX bars.** This is a real choice with costs on every side, not an oversight to correct.

1. **yfinance for bars, IBKR for execution.** Free, and already proven across 499 ASX sessions. But unofficial, rate-limited, and it **returns fabricated bars on a 404** — the guard `run_asx_replay.py` carries exists because this nearly produced expectancy on invented prices. It also means the strategy reads one venue's prices and trades at another's.
2. **IBKR for both.** One source for bars and execution, so the strategy sees the prices it trades against. Costs the subscription, and needs an `IBHistorySource` written WITH PACING, plus the `Literal` and the resolver extended.
3. **A third vendor for bars.** The spec's original point stands and is still the strongest argument for the move: point-in-time ASX membership including delisted names is commercially available in a way US index membership is not, so **the survivorship caveat every research manifest currently carries could actually be removed.**

**Option 1 is what happens if nobody decides**, and it is the one nobody would choose deliberately. Decide it explicitly.

**Stage 3 — ASX trading rules.** The **$500 minimum marketable parcel** reaches position sizing directly and is implemented nowhere. Also tick sizes, T+2 settlement against cash reconciliation, and the opening and closing auctions against session logic written for a 13:30 UTC open.

**Stage 4 — regime re-sourcing.** All five macro series are US, and the two carrying most of the signal have no FRED-hosted Australian equivalent. **Do not start this until the ablation question is settled** — the 19 August run found every rail inside noise, so whether the regime gate earns its keep at all is open, and if it does not, this stage disappears entirely.
